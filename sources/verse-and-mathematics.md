# Verse and mathematics: primary sources and questions

The repo title **Synectic** takes its cue from James Joseph Sylvester, who used the term in his discussion of verse. The point is not just a biographical curiosity: he treated patterns of language as things that could be investigated formally, while his mathematical writing likewise invented vocabulary and forms for working with new relationships.

## Primary texts

1. **James Joseph Sylvester, *The Laws of Verse; or, Principles of Versification Exemplified in Metrical Translations* (1870).** Its contents list **“Synectic, the Author's right-of-way into the Realm of Rhythm”** and **“‘Synectic’ explained”** at p. 64. [Digitized book and metadata (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_laws_of_verse,_or,_Principles_of_versification_exemplified_in_metrical_translations_-_together_with_an_annotated_reprint_of_the_inaugural_presidential_address_to_the_Mathematica_(IA_TheLawsOfVerse).pdf).
2. **Edgar Allan Poe, *The Rationale of Verse* (1848)**, an exploration of rhythm, equality of time, and quantitative scansion. Compare its theory with Sylvester's, without assuming that Sylvester's was derived from it. [1848 text (Edgar Allan Poe Society)](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/ratlvrs1.htm).
3. **J. J. Sylvester, *Collected Mathematical Papers*.** For the 1850 mathematical use of *matrix*, consult volume 1, p. 150, and compare [bibliographic reference and quotation (MacTutor)](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Miller/mathword/m/).
