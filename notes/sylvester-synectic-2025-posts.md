## 5 January 2025 — Sylvester's meeting of mathematics and verse

The screenshots quote or summarize **Daniel Brown, _The Poetry of Victorian Scientists: Style, Science and Nonsense_ (Cambridge University Press, 2013), chapter 9, “James Joseph Sylvester: the calculus of forms” (pp. 207–233)**.

- One post opens with **“My chief business is with Synectic.”** It presents Sylvester as giving his mathematics and poetry comparable importance.
- The term concerns the *subtly variable, continuous sound patterns of verse*. The analogy offered is to the motion of points tracing mathematical curves and to differential calculus.
- Another post reproduces Brown's report of Sylvester's geographical metaphor: Synectic descends from **“the Alps of Cauchy and Riemann”** into the poetic domain of Milton and Byron, forming a crossing point between literary scholarship and mathematical science.
- A continuation likens the term to alpine water moving across a landscape and invokes the mathematical **“Grand Continuum”** as a fertilizing reservoir for analysis. Treat this as a **metaphor** whose textual sources and mathematical meaning need to be established, not a theorem.
- Another describes three channels into which Synectic flows: **anastomosis, symptosis, phonetic syzygy**. The post singles out **syzygy** as central to Sylvester's poetics.

**Important correction to previous notes:** This is stronger than two unrelated homonymous terms. In the passage Brown cites, Sylvester **deliberately evokes Cauchy and Riemann** in describing Synectic. This is a literary and intellectual bridge; it is *not* evidence that the prosodic construct and the complex-function concept are mathematically equivalent.

## 15 January 2025 — Poe, and a separate YouTube comment lead

- A post links to **Edgar Allan Poe, “The Rationale of Verse”**, with the [Edgar Allan Poe Society publication history](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/perov.htm).
- The screenshot supplies location/year metadata “Philadelphia · 1842”. **That is not proof of the original publication date.** The _Rationale of Verse_ appeared in **1848**; related earlier Poe material includes **“Notes Upon English Verse” (1843)**.
- At the bottom of the screenshot a **partly visible** post says: “YouTube user Jacob Lus: Cauchy called holomorphic functions «synectic» until 1875”. The name is truncated/uncertain; no YouTube video title or comment URL is visible. **Do not use this as evidence of a Borcherds lecture or a timestamp.**
- Historical qualification: **Cauchy died in 1857.** The **1875** date relates to terminology in **Briot and Bouquet's second edition**, not Cauchy personally changing what he called functions in 1875. Their first edition uses _synectique_ (1859); their second edition uses _holomorphe_ (1875).
