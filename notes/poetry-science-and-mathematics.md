# Poetry, science, and mathematics

A reading dossier for **synectic**, assembled 2026-10-15. The purpose is to compare primary writings and concrete arguments, not assume that poetry obeys a particular mathematical law.

## Daniel Brown — Victorian scientists as poets

**Daniel Brown, _The Poetry of Victorian Scientists: Style, Science and Nonsense_** (Cambridge University Press, 2013).

- [Book and contents (publisher)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/poetry-of-victorian-scientists/8BE8F243531D4CEF0B74AD48EE3B93C2).
- Chapter 8: “James Joseph Sylvester: the romance of space,” pp. 183–206.
- Chapter 9: “James Joseph Sylvester: the calculus of forms,” pp. 207–233.
- Other central figures and topics in the contents: William Rowan Hamilton, Edward Lear, James Clerk Maxwell, John Tyndall; scientific imagination, puns, analogies, and nonsense.

This is **secondary scholarship**. Record page-specific claims, then compare them with the scientists' surviving writings. In particular, avoid reading the relationship between algebraic forms and poetic form into Sylvester without quotations and mathematical examples.

## Edgar Allan Poe — theory of verse

**Edgar Allan Poe, “The Rationale of Verse”** (first published in two parts, _Southern Literary Messenger_, October and November 1848).

- [Text of the 1848 first installment](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/ratlvrs1.htm).
- [Publication and edition history, with links to part II](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/perov.htm).
- An earlier related text is **“Notes Upon English Verse”** (_The Pioneer_, March 1843): [comparative edition](https://www.eapoe.org/works/cmprs/perovc03.htm).
- Related but different: **“The Philosophy of Composition”** (1846), on composition and technique rather than a complete system of prosody.

Poe declares in the introduction that “nine tenths ... appertain to the mathematics.” Investigate what this amounts to: timing of syllables, stress, equal intervals, metrical feet, rhyme, divisions and pauses. Keep apart **his proposals**, **facts about actual speech**, and **a formal model we might build**.

The title remembered as “algebra of poetry” or “reason of rhyme” is likely this essay, but do not substitute those phrases as an alternative bibliographic title.

## James Joseph Sylvester — mathematics and versification

**J. J. Sylvester, _The Laws of Verse; or, Principles of Versification Exemplified in Metrical Translations_** (London: Longmans, Green, 1870), accompanied by an annotated version of his address to the Mathematical and Physical Section of the British Association at Exeter in 1869.

- [Digitized original](https://archive.org/details/lawsofverseorpri00sylviala).
- [Google Books edition and contents](https://books.google.com/books?id=U-0NAAAAYAAJ).
- [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7042707M/The_laws_of_verse).

Passages for close reading:

- **p. 64:** “Synectic,” including an explanation of the term. The printed table of contents explicitly marks this passage.
- **p. 67:** rhythmical rests; **p. 69:** imaginary rhythmical time.
- **pp. 44–45:** function of rhyme and Sylvester's laws of anastomosis and syzygy.
- **p. 99 onward:** address on mathematics and the mathematician, and the subsequent discussion of Kant and space.

These are **Sylvester's claims and terminology**, not present-day established metrics. Study whether his examples withstand alternate readings or recitation.

### Sylvester — collected mathematical work

**_The Collected Mathematical Papers of James Joseph Sylvester_**, edited by H. F. Baker (Cambridge University Press), **four volumes**:

| Volume | Papers dated | Published |

| II | 1854–1873 | 1908 |
| III | 1870–1883 | 1909 |
| IV | 1882–1897 | 1912 |

- [Edition record and links to scans](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7096170M).
- [Internet Archive scan, vol. I](https://archive.org/details/collectedmathem01sylvrich).
- [Internet Archive scan, vol. IV](https://archive.org/details/collectedmathema04sylvuoft).
- [Library catalog identifying the editor and four-volume set](https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000387520).

The mathematical papers are **not a substitute** for _The Laws of Verse_. Compare relevant papers on algebraic forms, invariants, partition theory, or geometry with Sylvester's explicit claims about verse; supply document, volume, page and exact equations before drawing connections.

Other Sylvester verse-related works to look up: _The Law of Continuity as Applied to Versification_ (1869), _Fliegende Blätter_ (1876), _Spring's Debut_ (1880). These titles are documented in [the Dictionary of Scientific Biography's Sylvester bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Sylvester.pdf); full texts and edition details require separate verification.

## Related literary counterpoint

**Alexander Pushkin, _Mozart and Salieri_** (written 1830, published 1832), includes Salieri's dramatic claim about testing harmony by algebra. Do not turn a fictional character's position into Pushkin's asserted view.

- [English translation to locate the passage](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Mozart_and_Salieri).
- Recheck the Russian original before quoting a particular English translation.

## Research tasks

1. Work through a **single** example from Poe's prosody, marking what is defined and what can actually be measured.
2. Work through a corresponding example from Sylvester, noting his prescribed rests, timing, rhyme and sound connections.
3. Document where their accounts agree or contradict; assess them against readings by different speakers.
4. Identify a precise Sylvester mathematical paper and ask whether any formal relationship to his poetic vocabulary is demonstrable, not merely verbal.
5. Compare Brown's scholarly interpretation with passages in Sylvester's originals, preserving page numbers.
6. Keep history of the word **synectic** and the possible Richard Borcherds lecture in [the separate note](synectic-and-borcherds.md).
