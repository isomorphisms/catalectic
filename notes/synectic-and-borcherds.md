# “Synectic”: Sylvester, complex analysis, and the Borcherds lecture lead

Status: **two historical uses verified in printed sources; the specific Richard E. Borcherds lecture remains unidentified** (2026-10-15).

## 1. Sylvester's _synectic_ in prosody

In **J. J. Sylvester, _The Laws of Verse_ (1870), p. 64**, the contents identify “Synectic, the Author's right-of-way into the Realm of Rhythm” and “‘Synectic’ explained.”

- [Original edition, scanned](https://archive.org/details/lawsofverseorpri00sylviala).
- [Alternative scanned copy with transcribed contents](https://archive.org/details/TheLawsOfVerse).
- [Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books?id=U-0NAAAAYAAJ).

He distinguishes aspects of rhythm called **metric**, **chromatic**, and **synectic**. The synectic aspect concerns **continuity of sound** as distinct from discontinuous metrical measures; Brown's index lists it under prosody. Contemporary description of the distinction appears in [J. S. Stuart-Glennie, _Isis and Osiris_ (1878), p. 113](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1878__stuart-glennie___isis_and_osiris.pdf). For a full technical definition, quote Sylvester's own p. 64 and surrounding passage rather than relying on later summaries.

This belongs with [notes on Sylvester, Poe, and Victorian scientist-poets](poetry-science-and-mathematics.md).

## 2. _Synectique_ in nineteenth-century complex analysis

A **separate usage** appears in nineteenth-century French complex analysis:

- **Charles Briot and Jean-Claude Bouquet, _Théorie des fonctions doublement périodiques et, en particulier, des fonctions elliptiques_ (1859)**: use **_fonction synectique_**. Their section §10 (p. 11) treats a function that is **monodrome** (single-valued/path-independent in the relevant sense) and **monogène** (complex differentiable). [Digitized 1859 volume](https://archive.org/details/thoriedesfonctdo00briouoft); [alternative Google Books scan](https://books.google.com/books?id=OPYGAAAAcAAJ).
- **Briot and Bouquet, _Théorie des fonctions elliptiques_, second edition (1875)**: use **_fonction holomorphe_** instead; see §15, pp. 14–15. [Digitized original](https://archive.org/details/thoriedesfonct00briouoft).
- The term _synectique_ is also historically attributed to **Augustin-Louis Cauchy**; verify original Cauchy passages separately before assigning dates or a precise original definition. See [historical discussion with edition comparisons](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/3009108/why-is-it-called-a-holomorphic-function).

A modern holomorphic function on an open set is a complex-differentiable function. The older nomenclature is not automatically identical in every detail: check the historical authors' assumptions (single-valuedness, domain, continuations) before translating terminology into modern definitions.

**Do not conflate Sylvester's rhythmic continuity with Cauchy's function-theoretic continuity.** They are an intriguing common word and possible conceptual comparison, not a demonstrated influence or mathematical equivalence.
