# Synectic

Notes on how mathematical concepts acquire their familiar forms, beginning with nineteenth-century mathematics and its connections to Victorian science and poetry.

## First questions

- **[Why do matrices have entries?](notes/determinants-to-matrices.md)** Why did determinants precede matrices as algebraic objects? What did Sylvester, Cayley, and their predecessors mean by an array, and how did these constructions differ from today's linear-map interpretation?
- **[Verse and mathematics](sources/verse-and-mathematics.md)** J. J. Sylvester's *The Laws of Verse*, Poe's *The Rationale of Verse*, and sources for their mathematical approaches to rhythm.

## Method

Read original definitions and worked examples first. Distinguish a historical author's actual construction from a modern reconstruction that makes it easier to understand. Keep alternatives visible rather than assume that today's textbook definition was inevitable.

For matrices, compare determinant-generating arrays, systems of equations, linear maps, bilinear forms, and exterior powers. Ask precisely what an entry measures and which features of its arrangement depend on a chosen basis.

The title refers to Sylvester's use of **synectic** in his discussion of verse, not the later creativity technique called *Synectics*.
